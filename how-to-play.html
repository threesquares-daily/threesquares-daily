⭐ Stars

Also known as star battle (one-star variant).

Rules. Place exactly one star in every row, every column and every outlined colour region. Two stars may never touch — not side by side and not diagonally.

Solving tips

  • Start with the smallest region. A one- or two-square region pins its star almost immediately.
  • Cross out around every star. Its row, its column, its region and the eight squares around it can’t hold another star. Turn on “auto-mark ×” in settings to do this for you.
  • Regions trapped in one line. If every free square of a region lies in a single row, that row’s star belongs to the region — cross out the rest of the row. Same for columns.
  • Look for squares that would wipe out a region. If placing a star on a square would cross out every remaining square of some region, that square can’t be a star.
  • Count. If two regions fit entirely inside two rows, those two rows’ stars are used up by those regions.

🔶 Duo

A binary-grid puzzle in the family of takuzu / binairo, with = and × clues.

Rules. Fill every square of the 6×6 grid with ● or ◆. Each row and each column holds three of each. No more than two identical symbols may sit next to each other in a line. A = between two squares means they match; a × means they differ. Shaded squares are given.

Solving tips

  • Pairs force their neighbours. Two ● side by side? The squares on both ends must be ◆.
  • Gaps force their middle. ● _ ● means the middle must be ◆.
  • Count to three. Once a line has three ●, everything else in it is ◆.
  • Chain the signs. = and × pass information along: if A = B and B × C, then A × C.
  • Try-and-see, one step deep. If putting ● in a square instantly breaks a rule nearby, it must be ◆.

🧵 Thread

A path puzzle: one line, every square, numbers in order.

Rules. Starting on 1, draw a single line that moves up, down, left or right and passes through every square exactly once, visiting the numbers in order (1, 2, 3 …) and ending on the highest number.

Solving tips

  • Corners first. A corner square has only two exits, so the line must use both (unless it’s the start or the end).
  • Don’t strand a square. Any square left with only one free neighbour must be the end of the line.
  • Follow the numbers. Consecutive numbers that are close together leave little room for detours between them.
  • Hug the edges. Lines that skip a strip along the border usually make it unreachable later.
  • In the app, drag back to undo a step, or tap any square on your line to cut it there.
